Section 6

Sales call script

Use this structure for your 20–30 minute discovery call. The goal is not to explain the technology — it's to make the client feel the pain of their current situation, then show them the exit.

Stage 1 — Open with a question, not a pitch (2 mins)
"Before I show you anything, I want to understand how you're currently handling customer messages on WhatsApp. Like — what happens when someone texts you at 9pm asking about pricing?"

→ Let them answer. They'll either say "we miss it" or "I reply when I can." Both are your opening.
Stage 2 — Quantify the pain (5 mins)
"How many WhatsApp messages would you say you get in a day on average? And roughly what percentage do you think you're not replying to quickly?"

→ Now do the math with them out loud. "So if you're getting 20 messages a day and missing 5 of them, and each booking is worth ₹500... that's ₹2,500 a day, ₹75,000 a month, just from WhatsApp. And that's before accounting for no-shows."

→ Don't say the number yourself. Guide them to say it.
Stage 3 — Show the demo (10 mins)
"Can I show you what this looks like in practice? I've got a live demo set up for a salon — same kind of business as yours."

→ Open your portfolio demo. Type a message as the customer. Let them watch the bot reply in 2 seconds. Ask: "What would you say if a customer sent that to your WhatsApp right now?"

"This is running 24/7, knows everything about the business, and escalates to you if anything needs your personal touch."
Stage 4 — Anchor the comparison, not the price (3 mins)
"What does a front-desk staff member cost you per month? Including salary, bonuses, leave days?"

→ They'll say ₹15,000–25,000. You say:

"This does everything they do on WhatsApp — and it's on at 2am when they're not. My monthly fee is ₹83,000 ($1,000). But unlike a staff member, the moment you feel it's not adding value, you can cancel. No notice period, no severance."
Stage 5 — Remove all risk (2 mins)
"Here's what I suggest: I'll set it up for your business specifically, using your actual prices and services. Run it for 30 days. If it doesn't recover at least twice its cost in bookings or saved time, I'll refund the setup fee. The risk is entirely mine."

→ This close works because the math is already on your side. You've shown them they're losing ₹48,000/month. Your fee is ₹83,000. A 2x recovery bar is easy.
Section 7

Objection handling — with data

Every objection is a question in disguise. Answer the real question underneath it.

"₹83,000 is too expensive. I can hire someone for less."
A WhatsApp manager you hire works 8 hours a day and costs you ₹15,000–25,000/month — that's real. But they're asleep when 40% of your enquiries come in (evenings and weekends). They get sick. They resign. They can handle one conversation at a time. This bot handles all your messages simultaneously, at 2am on a Sunday, every day. The comparison isn't fair to the employee — this is a different category of tool entirely. When you factor in night and weekend coverage, you'd need 3 shifts of staff to match what one bot does.
"My customers prefer talking to a real person."
That's true for complex problems — which is exactly why the bot escalates those to you. But 54% of consumers now prefer WhatsApp bots for basic customer service queries like hours, pricing, and booking. They don't want to wait. They want an answer in 30 seconds. The bot handles that. You handle the relationships. And when you do respond personally to a complex question, it actually feels more special because it's you, not a routine reply.
"What if the bot says something wrong?"
The bot only answers from the knowledge base we build together — your exact prices, your exact services, your exact policies. It's not making things up. And any question it can't answer confidently gets flagged to you immediately with the full conversation context. In 6 months of testing across similar businesses, less than 2% of messages required a correction. You'll also get a full conversation log every day so you can see exactly what it said.
"Can't I just set up a WhatsApp Business auto-reply myself?"
You can — and it'll send a "We'll get back to you soon" message to everyone regardless of what they asked. That's not a reply, that's a delay. What I'm building actually reads what the customer wrote, understands whether they're asking about pricing or wanting to book or complaining, and gives them a specific, helpful answer. The difference is 78% of customers buy from the first business that responds meaningfully. A generic auto-reply doesn't count as a response in their mind.
"I'm not sure I need this right now."
Totally fair — let me ask you one thing. Every month you wait, you're losing the messages you're already not replying to. 85% of those customers won't come back. We've estimated that's roughly ₹40,000–50,000/month for a business your size. That's not a future cost. That's already happening. The only question is whether you want to keep paying that invisible bill, or redirect it.
Section 8

The three reasons the retainer model works for the client

This is the logic to have in your head — and to share when they ask why it's monthly rather than a one-time fee.

1 The business changes — the bot must change with it

Pricing goes up. New services get added. Holiday hours change. Promotions run. A one-time build becomes stale within weeks. The retainer covers continuous updates so the bot always reflects the real business. Without it, you'd either have to charge separately for every small update (which feels nickel-and-dime) or the client gets an increasingly inaccurate bot that starts frustrating customers.

2 Uptime and performance need ongoing monitoring

The Meta WhatsApp API has occasional breaking changes. The AI model gets updated. The webhook server needs monitoring. If the bot goes down at 11pm on a Friday and nobody notices until Monday, the client has lost a full weekend of after-hours messages. The retainer means someone (you) is responsible for it working — not just someone who built it once and left.

3 Monthly insights are where the real value compounds

Every month, you have a full log of every customer conversation. You can tell the client: "Your most-asked question last month was about parking — let's add a Google Maps link to the bot reply." Or: "You had 47 enquiries about a service you don't offer — you might want to add it." This turns you from a vendor into a trusted advisor. That relationship is what makes clients stay for years, not months.

The bottom line

Your client is already losing ₹40,000–50,000 a month in missed messages. You're offering to stop that bleed for ₹83,000/month, with a 30-day risk-free trial. The maths aren't close. The only reason someone says no is because they don't believe it yet — and that's a demo problem, not a price problem.
